Social Security Claiming Decision Checklist

By Jasper Saunders • Educational content only

When to claim Social Security is one of the most consequential decisions in retirement planning. Claiming earlier provides income sooner but reduces the monthly benefit for life. Delaying increases the monthly amount but requires other resources to bridge the gap.

Use this checklist to gather the key facts and questions before you decide.

1. Gather Your Personal Numbers

  • Create or log into your my Social Security account and download your latest statement.
  • Note your Full Retirement Age (FRA) and the benefit amounts shown at age 62, FRA, and age 70.
  • If married, gather the same information for your spouse.
  • Note any earnings history gaps or years that may still improve your average if you continue working.

2. Understand the Trade-Offs

  • How much does the monthly benefit increase for each year you delay past FRA (up to age 70)?
  • How much is the reduction if you claim before FRA?
  • What is your estimated break-even age under different claiming scenarios?
  • How does longevity risk factor into your thinking (family history, health)?

3. Coordinate with Other Income and Assets

  • Do you have enough portfolio or other income to delay claiming if that is the preferred strategy?
  • How does claiming age affect the amount you will need to withdraw from investments in the early years?
  • Are there tax considerations (taxation of benefits, impact on Medicare premiums, Roth conversion windows)?

Model different claiming ages inside the retirement calculator. Change the Social Security start age and amount, then re-run the stress test. Seeing how the portfolio is affected under different claiming choices is often more clarifying than abstract rules.

4. Consider Spousal and Survivor Issues

  • If married, how do the two claiming decisions interact?
  • What happens to benefits if one spouse dies (survivor benefits)?
  • Is there a strategy that better protects the surviving spouse?

5. Check for Special Situations

  • Do you have earnings that could trigger the earnings test if you claim before FRA while still working?
  • Are you eligible for benefits on a former spouse’s record?
  • Are there public pension offsets (WEP or GPO) that may reduce benefits?
  • Have you reviewed the latest rules, as details can change?

6. Make and Document the Decision

  • Write down the chosen claiming age(s) and the main reasons.
  • Note any conditions that would cause you to revisit the decision.
  • Set a calendar reminder for the application window if you are delaying.

Closing

There is no single correct claiming age for everyone. The better approach is to gather accurate personal numbers, understand the trade-offs, test the impact on the overall plan, and then choose deliberately. Revisit the decision if health, work, or family circumstances change significantly.
