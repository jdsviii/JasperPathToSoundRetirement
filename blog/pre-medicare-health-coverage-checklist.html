Pre-Medicare Health Coverage Checklist

By Jasper Saunders • Educational content only

If you retire before age 65, you will need health coverage until Medicare eligibility begins. This gap can be one of the largest and most uncertain costs in early retirement. Planning for it early reduces surprises.

1. Confirm Your Timeline

  • What is your planned retirement date?
  • When do you (and your spouse, if applicable) become eligible for Medicare?
  • How many months or years of gap coverage will you need?

2. Explore Available Options

  • COBRA continuation from your employer plan (typically 18 months, sometimes longer in special cases). Note the full cost.
  • Marketplace (ACA) plans - check eligibility for premium tax credits based on projected income.
  • Spouse’s employer plan, if available.
  • Retiree health coverage from a former employer, if offered.
  • Private individual policies or short-term plans (understand limitations carefully).
  • Health Care Sharing Ministries or other alternatives (review risks and coverage gaps).

3. Estimate Real Costs

  • Premiums for each viable option.
  • Deductibles, out-of-pocket maximums, and expected usage.
  • Prescription drug costs under each option.
  • Impact of income on Marketplace subsidies (and how retirement account withdrawals may affect that income).

Include realistic health insurance premiums and out-of-pocket estimates in the spending side of your retirement calculator projections. Underestimating this cost is a common source of plan stress in early retirement.

4. Check Special Enrollment and Timing Rules

  • When does coverage from your employer end?
  • What are the enrollment windows for COBRA, Marketplace, or other plans?
  • Are there penalties or waiting periods to avoid?

5. Plan the Transition to Medicare

  • Mark the Medicare Initial Enrollment Period on your calendar (generally the 7-month window around your 65th birthday).
  • Decide whether you will use Original Medicare + Medigap + Part D or a Medicare Advantage plan.
  • Understand how your pre-Medicare coverage ends relative to Medicare start date to avoid gaps.

Closing

Health coverage between employer plans and Medicare is a solvable problem when addressed early. Gather options, estimate full costs, and build those costs into your retirement spending plan. Clarity here reduces one of the larger sources of early-retirement pressure.
